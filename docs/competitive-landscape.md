### What this comparison suggests for Form

The pages make different kinds of decision information visible: material and fit, measurement help, certification or production details, and styling suggestions. In this three-product sample, each page lists a 100% cotton shirt, but gives shoppers different specifics: Supima® or Pima cotton, organic-content certification, or fiber weight and production origin. Those are seller-published product details, not independent tests. A fiber label alone does not establish how a particular shirt feels or wears over time, and the presence of a sustainability-related material claim does not prove that a product is broadly “sustainable.”

The learner chose **fabric composition** as the first evidence topic for Form. A useful next source would be the actual product listing, supplier specification, or garment label for the shirt being proposed. Form could then show the verified composition and any supporting certification or sourcing information, while marking anything unavailable. The current Form demo has no such documentation. The fictional $28 demo price is not a chosen real price and should not be used to position Form against these products.

## Persona hypothesis

**Low-key coffee-shop visitor** is a fictional planning persona based only on the learner's stated audience idea. It is an assumption to guide a first page, not a real interview participant or validated customer profile.

- **Situation:** Going to a coffee shop or another everyday outing.
- **Goal:** Find a simple white tee that feels considered and distinctive without a prominent logo.
- **Concern:** Wants to know what the shirt is made of and whether its quality is dependable; the actual product evidence has not been supplied.
- **Decision the page should support:** Whether the design and available product information are enough to choose the shirt.
- **Information gap:** Fabric, fit, care, and durability need verified product information before the page can answer those questions.

No age, occupation, demographic profile, or customer quote is invented here.

### Secondary persona hypothesis: the low-bulk layering shopper

This is a planning hypothesis based on the learner's stated need, not a real person or interview.

- **Situation:** Looking for a plain white T-shirt to wear under other clothes.
- **Goal:** A light-feeling, comfortable layer that sits close without adding much bulk.
- **Concern:** Cannot judge fabric weight or fit from the current demo; both are marked “Not supplied.”
- **Decision the page should support:** Whether verified fabric-weight and garment-measurement information suggests the shirt could work as a low-bulk layer.

These are the shopper's desired qualities, not verified qualities of Form's shirt.

## Draft agile user stories

These stories turn the persona's stated concerns into possible product-page needs. They are proposals for discussion, not research findings.

### US-01 — See product facts together

**As** someone choosing a white tee for everyday wear, **I want** verified material, fit, and care information in one place, **so that** I can judge whether it suits me without searching across multiple pages.

**Acceptance notes:** Show the exact supplied facts with a source or product documentation; label missing facts plainly; avoid unsupported comfort, quality, or longevity promises.

### US-02 — Choose a size with less guesswork

**As** a shopper deciding which size to try, **I want** a fit description and garment measurements, **so that** I can compare them with a shirt I already own.

**Acceptance notes:** Provide real measurements and explain how they are taken; identify model size/measurements if model imagery is used; mark classroom sample data as fictional.

### US-03 — Evaluate a sustainability statement

**As** a shopper who cares about how a shirt is made, **I want** any sustainability statement tied to specific material or certification evidence, **so that** I can understand what the statement covers.

**Acceptance notes:** Name the specific material or standard and link to a credible source; do not use a broad “sustainable” claim when evidence or scope is unclear.

### US-04 — Judge a tee as a layer

**As** someone choosing a lightweight-feeling layer, **I want** verified fabric weight and garment measurements, **so that** I can judge whether it may sit comfortably under other clothes without adding much bulk.

**Acceptance notes:** Use supplier documentation or garment measurements as the source; keep unavailable facts marked “Not supplied”; do not promise comfort or fit from fabric weight alone.

## Sources and limits

The comparison uses the linked brands' own product pages, not independent product testing. Listed prices are a dated snapshot; promotions, stock, and product details can change. Positioning and audience descriptions are interpretations of page content, not verified customer segments. This comparison does not establish which product is better or validate demand for Form.
